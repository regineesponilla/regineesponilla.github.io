**Market Research + Strategic Customer Intelligence**  
**Project:** Technological University of the Shannon Live Project (TUS)  
**Client:** Crescent Shopping Centre, Limerick, Ireland  
**Role:** Lead Analyst / Research Consultant  
**Timeline:** Jan 2025 – Apr 2025 (4 months)  
**Status:** Completed | Results: 305-respondent survey, actionable insights implemented, client adopted recommendations

---

## CONTEXT

Crescent Shopping Centre is a major retail destination in Limerick, Ireland, serving the greater mid-west region. The centre operates as a community hub—not just retail, but dining, entertainment, and social gathering space for customers across age groups, income levels, and life stages.

In early 2025, the centre's management wanted a deeper understanding of their customer base: Who shops here? What brings them? What could they be doing better? The centre had strong foot traffic but wanted to make data-driven decisions about tenant mix, amenities, and marketing.

Crescent engaged Technological University of the Shannon for a comprehensive customer research project. As the lead analyst on the consulting team, my responsibilities included:

- Designing and executing a comprehensive customer survey
- Analyzing 305+ respondent data across demographics, behavior, and preferences
- Identifying patterns in customer satisfaction, pain points, and opportunities
- Synthesizing insights into actionable strategic recommendations
- Presenting findings to centre management and helping them prioritize initiatives

This was a high-stakes project: the insights would directly inform millions of euros in capital investment decisions and ongoing tenant/operational strategy.

---

## THE CHALLENGE

**The Research Question**

Crescent's management knew their basics (which stores were busy, which were quiet), but they didn't understand:
- **Customer profiling:** Who actually shops here? Are they whom we think they are?
- **Behavioral patterns:** When do customers visit? How long do they stay? Do they come for specific stores or to browse?
- **Unmet needs:** What services do customers want that we don't offer?
- **Competitive positioning:** How do we compare to other shopping destinations in the region?
- **Sustainability interest:** Is environmental responsibility important to our customer base?

Without this data, every operational decision was based on guesswork. Should they invest in a food court expansion? Add more parking? Improve children's facilities? Increase digital engagement?

Crescent needed evidence to guide these million-euro decisions.

---

## THE RESEARCH METHODOLOGY

### Survey Design

I designed a comprehensive 40-question survey covering:

**Customer Demographics (8 questions)**
- Age, gender, employment status, household composition
- Geographic origin (where customers traveled from)
- Frequency of visits and typical visit timing

**Shopping Behavior (9 questions)**
- Primary reasons for visiting
- Stores and categories most shopped
- Average spend and dwell time
- Visit patterns (weekly, monthly, seasonal)
- Transportation mode

**Customer Satisfaction (6 questions)**
- Overall experience rating
- Likes about the centre
- Dislikes and areas for improvement
- Accessibility considerations

**Environmental & Sustainability (8 questions)**
- Awareness of Crescent's sustainability efforts
- Interest in eco-friendly features (EV charging, recycling, etc.)
- Importance of sustainability in shopping decisions

**Future Opportunities (9 questions)**
- Desired new services and stores
- Content and engagement preferences
- Event and workshop interest
- Communication channel preferences

### Data Collection

- **Survey method:** Microsoft Forms (online + QR code printed at centre)
- **Sample size:** 305 completed responses
- **Collection period:** 8 weeks
- **Sampling approach:** Intercept surveys + online promotion (ensuring diverse respondent base)

### Analysis Approach

- **Quantitative analysis:** Frequency distributions, cross-tabulations, correlation analysis
- **Qualitative analysis:** Open-ended response coding and thematic analysis (word clouds showing top requested improvements)
- **Segment analysis:** Age-group breakdowns, geography-based segments, visit-frequency cohorts
- **Visualization:** Charts, infographics, heatmaps for presentation to stakeholders

## KEY FINDINGS

### Customer Profile

**Demographics (305 respondents):**
- **Gender:** 65% female, 35% male (slightly female-skewed, typical for retail destinations)
- **Age distribution:** Top groups are 25-34 (26%) and 18-24 (25%) — younger demographic, strong millennial presence
- **Employment:** 49% employed full-time, 14% part-time, 15% students, remainder retired/self-employed
- **Geography:** 36% from Limerick County, 29% from Limerick City, 10% from Claire, 7% from Tipperary — primarily mid-west region

**Key insight:** Crescent attracts a younger, working-age demographic with strong local (but not hyperlocal) reach. Potential for digital engagement and experience-based offerings vs. pure retail.

### Shopping Behavior

**Visit Patterns:**
- **Frequency:** 51% visit weekly, 25% visit monthly, 12% occasional
- **Typical timing:** 40% visit in mornings, 37% afternoons, 22% evenings (morning preference for peak traffic)
- **Day of week:** Wednesday is most popular (21%), followed by Saturday (17%)
- **Dwell time:** Average ~90 minutes per visit (healthy engagement)

**Reasons for Visiting (Top 5):**
1. Clothes/Fashion (26%)
2. Groceries/Essentials (12%)
3. Shoes/Footwear (10%)
4. Dining/Cafés (9%)
5. General shopping/Browsing (8%)

**Key insight:** Fashion is the primary driver, but grocery shopping shows there's a real convenience component. Cafés are underperforming relative to demand.

**Transportation:**
- 62% arrive by car
- 15% by bus
- 8% walking
- 8% other (taxi, cycling)

**Key insight:** Car-dependent customer base makes parking critical and EV infrastructure valuable.

### Satisfaction Metrics

**Overall Experience Rating: 4.49/5 (Excellent)**
- 61% rated experience as Level 5 (excellent)
- 29% rated Level 4 (good)
- 8% rated Level 3 (average)
- Only 2% gave below-average ratings

**Most Appreciated Services/Features:**
1. Variety of stores (31%)
2. Parking availability (23%)
3. Cleanliness (18%)
4. Store layout/navigability (14%)

**Areas for Improvement:**
1. Food court options (27% — top complaint)
2. Store variety (18%)
3. Accessibility features (10%)
4. Customer service training (6%)

**Key insight:** Crescent's basics are solid (people love the variety and clean environment), but there's one glaring gap: food and beverage experience.

### Sustainability Awareness & Interest

**Awareness:**
- **52% aware** of Crescent's sustainability efforts (balanced)
- **48% unaware** (significant opportunity for better communication)

**Recognized Sustainability Efforts:**
- Recycling stations (46% aware)
- EV charging stations (27% aware)
- Energy-efficient lighting (14% aware)

**Desire for Future Eco-Friendly Features (Critical Finding):**
- **72% want EV charging stations** (extremely high demand)
- **68% want recycling/composting access** (critical)
- **35% say sustainability affects purchasing decisions** (meaningful segment)

**Most Requested Eco-Initiatives:**
1. Discounts for reusable shopping bags (31%)
2. More recycling bins (28%)
3. Sustainable product promotions (20%)
4. Workshops on sustainable living (11%)

**Key insight:** Sustainability is NOT a niche interest—a third of customers actively consider it in shopping decisions, and nearly 3 in 4 want specific eco-features. This is a major unmet opportunity.

## STRATEGIC RECOMMENDATIONS

### Immediate Priorities (6 months)

1. **Expand Food & Beverage Offering**
   - 27% of feedback mentions food court as primary gap
   - Recommend: New casual dining space, food court vendors, café expansion
   - Expected impact: Increase dwell time, drive repeat visits, increase customer spend

2. **Communicate Sustainability Efforts**
   - 48% of customers unaware of existing initiatives
   - Recommend: In-centre signage, email campaigns, social media, QR codes at recycling stations
   - Expected impact: Increase perceived value, differentiate from competitors, build loyalty

3. **Expand EV Charging**
   - 72% of customers want this feature
   - Currently limited infrastructure
   - Recommend: Phase in additional EV charging spaces across car park
   - Expected impact: Attract eco-conscious customers, future-proof parking infrastructure

### Medium-Term Initiatives (6-12 months)

4. **Optimize Store Mix**
   - 18% want more store variety, particularly in non-fashion categories
   - Data shows opportunity gaps: Home & furniture (2%), Health & wellness (4%)
   - Recommend: Strategic tenant recruitment targeting currently underserved categories

5. **Enhance Accessibility**
   - 10% of feedback mentions accessibility concerns (wheelchairs, families, elderly customers)
   - Recommend: Accessibility audit, targeted improvements (ramps, facilities, parking designations)

6. **Launch Sustainability-Focused Marketing**
   - Segment strategy: 35% of customers care about sustainability; target them with eco-narrative
   - Recommend: "Crescent Cares" campaign, sustainability event calendar, retailer partnerships

## LEARNINGS & REFLECTION

### What I Learned

**1. Ask the right questions to uncover hidden insights**
- We asked about "desired improvements" (expecting minor complaints)
- The data revealed a massive unmet need (food court) and a major opportunity (sustainability)
- **Lesson:** Open-ended research captures what customers actually care about, not what companies assume they care about

**2. Segment analysis reveals opportunity**
- Overall satisfaction was 4.49/5, which looked great
- But 27% of feedback specifically mentioned food court, 72% wanted EV charging
- Aggregated scores hide critical problems if you don't dig into segments
- **Lesson:** Always segment; look at the distribution, not just the average

**3. Sustainability is mainstream, not niche**
- Assumed sustainability would be interesting to 10-15% of customers
- Data showed 35% actively use it in shopping decisions, 72% want it as an option
- **Lesson:** Don't make assumptions about customer values; measure them

**4. Research is only valuable if it's acted upon**
- I worried the 305-respondent survey might be "nice research but not actionable"
- Crescent's immediate investment in recommendations proved research drives business decisions
- **Lesson:** Frame findings in business terms (market opportunity, revenue impact, competitive differentiation)

### What I'd Do Differently

**1. Add follow-up study design into recommendations**
- Proposed recommendations without baseline metrics to measure success
- Should have recommended: "After food court opens, re-survey customers to measure dwell time change"

**2. Include competitor research**
- Surveyed Crescent customers but didn't benchmark against other regional shopping destinations
- Would have strengthened recommendations with data on "Crescent vs. regional alternatives"

**3. Conduct deeper customer segmentation**
- Divided customers into age groups, frequency
- Could have created richer personas (e.g., "Busy Professionals," "Family Shoppers," "Social Visitors") for more targeted strategy
