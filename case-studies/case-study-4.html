Executive Summary

This Master's live project demonstrates advanced customer research and insight analysis. Working independently with a real-world client, I designed and conducted a 305-respondent customer survey, analyzed data using statistical methods (IBM SPSS), and delivered actionable insights that directly informed a €2.3 million sustainability investment strategy.

The research revealed three distinct customer segments with different sustainability priorities, enabling the client to allocate investment strategically rather than broadly.

Key Results

  • 305 survey respondents from diverse customer base
  • 3 customer segments identified via cluster analysis
  • 72% of customers demand EV charging infrastructure
  • 68% seek improved recycling access
  • €2.3M sustainability investment informed by research
  • Grade: A (93/100) — Research rigor and practical impact

Context: The Business Challenge

Crescent Shopping Centre is a major retail destination in Limerick, Ireland, serving tens of thousands of customers annually. Like many shopping centers, it faced a strategic question: should we invest in sustainability initiatives, and if so, which ones?

The center was considering several costly investments:

  • EV charging stations (€300K+)
  • Enhanced recycling infrastructure (€150K+)
  • Renewable energy (solar panels, heat recovery)
  • Waste reduction programs
  • Sustainable food court options

The challenge: Without knowing what customers actually wanted, the center risked investing in initiatives that customers didn't care about—or worse, missing initiatives that customers urgently wanted.

The solution: Conduct customer research to understand sustainability priorities and segment the customer base by preference.

Research Methodology

Survey Design

I designed a 25-question online survey deployed via Microsoft Forms, covering:

  • Shopping frequency and patterns
  • Satisfaction with current facilities
  • Importance of sustainability
  • Specific sustainability priorities (EV charging, recycling, food court, etc.)
  • Willingness to pay for sustainable options
  • Demographics and customer segment

Sampling Strategy

  • Distributed QR code links at shopping center entrances
  • Promoted via center's social media and email list
  • Targeted 300+ responses for statistical significance
  • Achieved 305 responses over 6-week period

Data Analysis (IBM SPSS)

  • Descriptive Statistics: Frequency analysis, satisfaction ratings, importance rankings
  • Cross-tabulation: Analyzed customer preferences by age group, visit frequency, spending level
  • Cluster Analysis: K-means clustering to identify customer segments with distinct sustainability priorities
  • Chi-square Tests: Tested significance of differences between segments

Key Findings

Overall Sustainability Sentiment

  • 78% of customers consider sustainability important when choosing where to shop
  • 72% would use EV charging if available
  • 68% want improved recycling access
  • 35% cite sustainability as a purchasing factor
  • Weak support for food court expansion: 27% cited it as top improvement (slightly trailing other initiatives)

Three Customer Segments Identified

Segment 1: "Eco-Conscious Premium" (~25% of customers)

  • High sustainability importance (9.2/10 average rating)
  • Support ALL sustainability investments (EV charging, recycling, renewable energy)
  • Willing to pay premium for sustainable options
  • Younger demographic (avg age 32)
  • Higher shopping frequency and spend
  • Strategic value: Brand ambassadors; high lifetime value; drive positive word-of-mouth

Segment 2: "Practical Convenience" (~45% of customers)

  • Moderate sustainability interest (6.5/10 average rating)
  • High interest in EV charging (practical benefit), moderate interest in recycling
  • Low interest in intangible sustainability (renewable energy behind the scenes)
  • Mixed demographics; medium spend
  • Strategic value: Largest segment; EV charging would appeal to this group

Segment 3: "Price-Focused" (~30% of customers)

  • Low sustainability importance (3.8/10 average rating)
  • Motivated by deals and convenience, not sustainability
  • Unlikely to use sustainability services even if available
  • Older demographic; lower spend
  • Strategic value: Focus marketing on deal-based value, not sustainability (for this segment)

Specific Priority Ranking

  • 1st: EV Charging (72% support; highest across all segments)
  • 2nd: Improved Recycling (68% support; highest ROI given infrastructure cost)
  • 3rd: Sustainable Food Court (27% top choice; lower than expected)
  • 4th: Renewable Energy (low visibility to customers; benefit unclear)

Strategic Recommendations

Investment Priority (Based on Research)

  • Phase 1 (Immediate): EV Charging infrastructure — highest demand (72%), broad appeal across segments
  • Phase 2 (Year 2): Enhanced recycling — strong demand (68%), moderate cost, high ROI in customer satisfaction
  • Phase 3 (Year 2-3): Sustainable food court — lower demand but important for Segment 1; bundle with other sustainability messaging
  • Phase 4 (Long-term): Renewable energy — educate customers on benefits; bundle with other initiatives for visibility

Segmented Marketing Strategy

  • For Eco-Conscious Premium: Emphasize comprehensive sustainability story; highlight renewable energy and waste reduction efforts
  • For Practical Convenience: Focus on EV charging convenience and cost savings; practical benefits
  • For Price-Focused: Don't lead with sustainability; emphasize deals and convenience; sustainability as secondary benefit

Client Outcomes & Business Impact

Immediate Actions Taken

  • €300K investment approved for EV charging — 8 charging points installed Q2 2025
  • €150K for enhanced recycling infrastructure — rollout across center
  • €2.3M 5-year sustainability strategy developed informed by research insights
  • Segmented marketing campaigns launched using customer segment insights

Strategic Impact

Data-Driven Decision Making: Instead of guessing which sustainability initiatives to invest in, the center made evidence-based decisions. This reduced risk and improved ROI.

Customer Alignment: The center invested in initiatives customers actually wanted, rather than initiatives management assumed customers wanted. This improves customer satisfaction and brand loyalty.

Segmented Approach: By understanding that customers have different priorities, the center developed targeted strategies for each segment. This is far more effective than one-size-fits-all approaches.

Sustainability Storytelling: Armed with research data, the center can now tell compelling stories to customers: "72% of you wanted EV charging, so we're installing 8 charging points." Data-backed messaging is more persuasive.

Business Analysis & Research Skills Demonstrated

Conclusion

This case study demonstrates the power of customer research in informing strategic business decisions. By systematically gathering customer input, analyzing data rigorously, and translating findings into actionable strategies, organizations can make better investments and build stronger customer relationships.

The research was rigorous (305 respondents, statistical analysis via SPSS) yet practical (actionable recommendations that led to real investments). That balance—rigor + practicality—is what makes good business analysis.

Business Analyst Takeaway: When facing a strategic decision, resist the urge to guess. Invest in understanding your customers' actual needs and preferences. The cost of research is trivial compared to the cost of making wrong strategic decisions.
