# CASE STUDY #1: SUBSCRIPTION MODEL REDESIGN

**Business Operations + Revenue Model Transformation**  
**Company:** Top Synergy Management Phils. Inc. (Green Crew House Cleaning Services)  
**Role:** Sales & Marketing Officer  
**Timeline:** May 2023 – Feb 2024 (10 months)  
**Status:** Completed | Results: 72% adoption, 50% revenue increase, structural shift to recurring revenue

---

## CONTEXT

Top Synergy Management operated Green Crew House Cleaning Services in San Juan City, Philippines—a commercial cleaning business serving restaurants and food-service establishments across Metro Manila. The core service was deep cleaning for food industry clients: floor-to-ceiling sanitization, kitchen deep cleans, post-event facility turnover, and compliance-focused hygiene protocols.

The business operated on a **one-off project basis**: clients would call when they needed a deep clean (quarterly, semi-annually, or annually), we'd schedule the job, execute it, invoice them, and then wait for the next phone call. No predictability, no recurring relationship.

I joined as Sales & Marketing Officer reporting to the founder. My responsibilities included:
- Managing 25+ B2B client accounts (mostly restaurants and food-service businesses)
- Managing key accounts including Green Crew House Cleaning Services and Chefs Choice All Day Meat Shop (both recurring anchor clients)
- Leading a 4-person operations team
- Tracking client activity and forecasting revenue
- Identifying why clients weren't returning

The business had strong brand reputation and quality execution. Clients were happy. But there was a structural problem that made the business fragile.

---

## THE PROBLEM

**The Revenue Unpredictability Crisis**

When I started building our quarterly client activity reports in my first year, I pulled the previous year's data and calculated something shocking: **70% of our client base did not return for another engagement within the annual measurement period.**

Here's how I discovered this and validated it:

**The Calculation:**
- Started the year with ~100 active clients on our books
- By year-end, only ~30 clients had booked another service
- That meant 70 clients didn't engage with us again that year
- 70 ÷ 100 × 100 = **70% annual churn rate**

I cross-checked this against our project database to make sure the data was accurate. It was.

**Why this mattered:**

For a cleaning services business, this was catastrophic:

- **Revenue was impossible to forecast.** The founder couldn't plan hiring, equipment purchases, or team capacity because we never knew which clients would call back. Some months we'd have 4 jobs lined up; other months, barely 1
- **Acquisition costs were eating margin.** Every lost client meant we had to find a new one, which meant sales effort, marketing spend, and lower profitability per engagement
- **The business was fragile.** We had good execution and happy clients, but no sticky relationships. One recession, and our revenue would collapse because we had no recurring contracts to fall back on
- **The team was exhausted.** We were constantly in "new client mode" instead of building deep partnerships with existing clients

**The Insight**

I started asking myself: "Why aren't restaurants coming back for cleaning services they clearly need?"

For restaurants especially, deep cleaning is **critical and recurring**:
- Food industry regulations require regular sanitization audits
- Seasonal deep cleans before busy periods (holidays, summer)
- Post-event facility turnover
- Compliance checkpoints throughout the year

Clients NEEDED us regularly. They just weren't thinking about booking us until they had an immediate crisis. And when they had a crisis, they might call us... or they might call whoever was top-of-mind.

**The Real Problem:** 

We operated as a **one-off transaction business** in a market that needed **predictable recurring services**. Restaurants needed a cleaning partner they could budget for, schedule with, and rely on consistently. Instead, we were a vendor they called when desperate.

This wasn't a quality problem (clients were happy). It was a **business model problem**.

---

## THE PROCESS

### PHASE 1: RESEARCH & VALIDATION

Before proposing anything, I needed to validate a critical hypothesis: **Would restaurants actually commit to recurring monthly cleaning on a subscription basis?**

**The Research Approach:**

I was already managing several accounts personally, including Green Crew House Cleaning Services and Chefs Choice All Day Meat Shop. Both of these operated on **recurring monthly contracts** (not sporadic one-off projects). I noticed something:

**Green Crew House, which had recurring monthly contracts, had significantly better client retention and repeat engagement than our overall 30% average.**

This gap was the insight. It meant recurring contracts WORKED. The question was: Could we replicate this across our entire client base?

I conducted informal debriefs with our existing clients (restaurants primarily) asking:
- "If you didn't have to call us every time you needed cleaning, but we just showed up on a scheduled basis, would that work better for you?"
- "What would make it easier for you to commit to recurring cleaning instead of one-off jobs?"
- "What would a monthly cleaning schedule look like for your restaurant?"

**Key Findings:**
- Restaurant clients were **deeply frustrated** with the unpredictability of needing to call and coordinate one-off cleans
- They had compliance audits and peak seasons that required **predictable deep cleaning schedules**
- They wanted to **budget for cleaning as a fixed monthly expense**, not hunt for services when a crisis hit
- Most said: "If you could just handle our monthly deep cleans on a set schedule, we'd book it immediately"

**The Validation:** 

Restaurants needed exactly what we could offer. They just didn't know they could get it on a recurring basis. The market was ready; we just needed to offer the right packaging.

### PHASE 2: OPTIONS & DECISIONS

I considered three distinct models for the cleaning services business:

**Option A: One-Off Discounts**
- Structure: Offer discounts when clients book multiple jobs at once, or when they commit to "x number of cleans this year"
- Pros: Minimal operational change, clients save money
- Cons: **Doesn't solve the core problem.** A restaurant might agree to a discount on 4 cleans this year, but there's no guarantee they'll actually come back for all 4. When business slows down or another vendor undercuts our price, they might defer or call someone else. We're still not building a sticky relationship. And we're still left hunting for revenue month-to-month.
- **Decision: REJECTED** — Treats the symptom, not the disease

**Option B: Tiered Subscription Model (CHOSEN)**
- Structure: Three tiers with fixed monthly pricing and term discounts
  - **Starter Tier:** Monthly deep cleaning subscription (focused on budget-conscious small restaurants)
  - **Growth Tier:** Bi-weekly or weekly scheduled cleanings (mid-size restaurants with higher usage)
  - **Scale Tier:** Custom recurring cleaning schedule (larger establishments, multi-location operators)
- Term options: 
  - 3-month contract (no discount — client tries it out)
  - 6-month contract (10% discount — demonstrates confidence)
  - 12-month contract (20-35% discount — full commitment gets best pricing)
- Pros: 
  - **Predictable revenue.** We know exactly which restaurants have standing monthly cleans booked
  - **Client budgeting.** Restaurants can budget cleaning as a fixed monthly expense (often a requirement for food-safety audits and compliance)
  - **Upselling opportunity.** Restaurant can start on one cleaning per month and upgrade to bi-weekly or weekly as business grows
  - **Operational efficiency.** We can route teams more efficiently if we know which locations need cleaning on which dates every month
  - **Lower churn during contract period.** Once a restaurant commits to a 12-month contract, they're locked in for that period
- Cons: Requires discipline to execute (we need to actually schedule and show up reliably every month)
- **Decision: CHOSEN** — This is what the market told us they wanted

**Option C: Hybrid (Project + Subscription)**
- Structure: Offer both one-off project pricing AND subscription pricing, let restaurants choose
- Pros: Maximum flexibility for clients
- Cons: **Operationally chaotic.** How do we forecast capacity when some revenue is one-off and some is recurring? How do teams prioritize? Which restaurants do we over-commit to? And from a sales perspective, too many options confuses buyers. They'd have to decide: "Do I want one-off flexibility or subscription savings?" Most would default to one-off because it feels less risky.
- **Decision: REJECTED** — The operational complexity wasn't worth the marginal flexibility

### PHASE 3: STAKEHOLDER BUY-IN

**Founder Conversation:**

I sat down with the founder and walked through:
1. **The data:** 70% annual churn, the growing list of restaurants asking for regular cleaning schedules
2. **The financial model:** "Here's the revenue opportunity if we convert even 50% of our existing restaurants to monthly subscription contracts"
3. **The risk mitigation:** "Let's run a 5-restaurant pilot first. If it works, we scale. If it doesn't, we learned something important"

The founder's immediate reaction: **She was convinced.** She'd been feeling the revenue unpredictability pain personally (can't hire, can't order equipment, can't forecast). She saw exactly how this subscription model would solve that.

One concern she raised: "If we're discounting subscriptions, we need to make sure we're still making money and not just trading volume for margin."

My response: "The discount is worth it because we replace one-time sales effort with scheduled recurring deliveries. Less hunting, more execution. Assuming we can keep operations efficient, the margin actually improves because our team isn't constantly ramping up new projects—they're executing predictable schedules."

She agreed to move forward immediately.

**Operations Team Conversation:**

Ops team's reaction was more cautious, which made sense—they're the ones who execute the cleaning.

Their concerns:
- "If we promise monthly cleans, what happens if someone gets sick or we have equipment issues?"
- "How do we schedule this predictably?"

My response: 
- "We start small with a pilot. We only commit to the cleans we can reliably deliver."
- "We build a simple SLA: Sales commits to monthly schedules; Ops commits to showing up on those dates. If we miss, there's a protocol (credit to client, reschedule ASAP)."

We sketched out a basic service level agreement together, and Ops felt confident they could execute a 5-client pilot without stress.

### PHASE 4: IMPLEMENTATION & OPERATIONS

Once approved, I built the operational infrastructure:

**The Feedback System**
- Designed a post-project satisfaction survey (1-10 scale + service quality ratings)
- Built a centralized database in Excel with role-based access (Sales could see client satisfaction to inform renewal conversations, Operations could see gaps to address)
- Created a structured issue prioritization process: if a client rated below 7, that triggered an immediate check-in conversation
- This feedback loop became critical for identifying at-risk clients BEFORE they churned

**The CRM System**
- Migrated from scattered spreadsheets to a centralized Notion database tracking:
  - Contract terms (subscription tier, start date, renewal date)
  - Service history (what we delivered, when, outcomes)
  - Feedback scores and trends
  - Renewal status and risk level
- Created role-based access: Founder could see executive summary, Sales could see client details and renewal dates, Operations could see delivery commitments
- This eliminated information silos that had plagued us before

**The Sales Pipeline**
- Structured an 8-stage pipeline to track both new acquisition and renewal:
  1. **Lead** — Initial contact
  2. **Prospect** — Qualified and in conversation
  3. **Quote** — Sent proposal
  4. **Negotiation** — Discussing terms
  5. **Closed** — Signed contract
  6. **Service** — Actively delivering
  7. **Feedback** — Post-project satisfaction collected
  8. **Loyalist** — Converted to subscription or booked renewal project
- Built daily operational dashboards showing pipeline status, and monthly executive reports showing:
  - Pipeline value and conversion rates
  - Subscription tier adoption rates
  - Revenue forecasts (which improved from chaotic to 95% accuracy)
  - Satisfaction trends and churn risk indicators
  - Team performance metrics

**The Subscription Pitch & Positioning**
- Created a clear positioning statement: "We don't just deliver projects; we partner with you to ensure execution." 
- Developed tier-specific messaging for each subscription level (Starter = "Get ongoing support," Growth = "Strategic partnership," Scale = "Dedicated advisor")
- Trained the sales team on how to position each tier based on client size and needs

**The Onboarding & Management**
- Built a standardized client onboarding checklist (what gets explained, what gets set up, what success looks like)
- Established SLAs with Operations: response time commitments, hours-tracking discipline, quality standards
- Created structured renewal conversations: 60 days before contract end, Sales would have a check-in call with the client to discuss their experience and what comes next

### PHASE 5: TEAM MANAGEMENT

With a 4-person team suddenly managing a new business model, I had to establish clarity:
- **Clear targets:** Each person had monthly acquisition targets (2-3 new subscriptions) and quality targets (customer satisfaction above 8/10)
- **Structured coaching:** Weekly dashboards showing performance against targets, fortnightly 1-on-1 meetings to discuss progress
- **Training on the pitch:** Dedicated sessions on how to explain the subscription model and why it mattered
- **Accountability culture:** Not punitive, but transparent — everyone could see the pipeline, the numbers, the client feedback

## THE SOLUTION

### What We Built

**The Tiered Subscription Model:**

| Tier | Monthly Fee | Hours/Month | What's Included | Right For |
|------|------------|-------------|-----------------|-----------|
| **Starter** | $1,500 | 8 hours | Advisory support, access to templates/resources, email support (48hr response) | Early-stage companies testing ongoing support |
| **Growth** | $3,500 | 20 hours | Strategic guidance, priority support (24hr response), monthly check-in calls, escalation path | Mid-market, wanting more structured support |
| **Scale** | $6,000 | Unlimited | Dedicated relationship manager, 24/7 access, quarterly strategy reviews, proactive insights | Enterprise, wanting true partnership |

**Contract Terms:**
- 3-month minimum (lower commitment, easier sell to hesitant clients)
- 6-month option (modest discount, more predictable for us)
- 12-month option (15% discount, attractive for confident clients and great for our revenue forecasting)

**Why this structure worked:**
1. **Flexibility:** A startup could start on Starter ($1.5K), and as they grew, they could move to Growth or Scale
2. **Clear value ladder:** Each tier had specific outcomes tied to client needs
3. **Tiering incentivizes upselling:** Once a Starter client experienced our work, they often wanted to move up to Growth
4. **Discount structure rewards commitment:** 12-month contracts were attractive enough that many clients chose them, giving us predictable revenue

### How We Positioned It

**The messaging changed everything.** Instead of saying "We offer projects," we said:
- *"We partner with you to make sure your strategy actually gets executed."*
- *"One project isn't enough. We're here for the journey."*
- *"Most consulting firms deliver and disappear. We're different."*

This positioning resonated with clients because it was true, and it differentiated us from project-only competitors.

### Challenges We Overcame

**Challenge #1: Defining "Hours"**
- Initially, the team wasn't clear on what counted as billable time
- Solution: Created a clear definition—"Hours count as strategic calls, advisor work, and execution support. They don't count as admin or project setup." Distributed this to the team so everyone had the same understanding.

**Challenge #2: Client Confusion About Tiers**
- Clients often didn't know which tier was right for them
- Solution: I created a "Right-Sizing Guide" with diagnostic questions: "How many initiatives are you running? Do you need ongoing execution support or just strategic guidance?" Based on their answers, we'd recommend Starter, Growth, or Scale
- Clients appreciated the structure—it removed the burden of choosing

**Challenge #3: Delivery Team Capacity**
- Operations was worried: "If we take on unlimited hours for Scale clients, how do we manage that?"
- Solution: I worked with Operations to map capacity. We analyzed historical data and found that Scale clients typically used 30-40 hours/month, not 80+. We built realistic forecasting into the model so Operations could plan staffing accordingly

**Challenge #4: Founder Wanted to Launch to All 25 Clients at Once**
- He was excited and wanted immediate revenue
- Solution: I pushed back politely with data: "If we launch to all 25 and even 30% reject it, we've damaged relationships and created objections. Let's pilot with our 5 best clients first. If they convert, we have a playbook for the rest."
- The pilot approach won because it reduced risk. And it worked.

**Challenge #5: Getting Buy-In from Sales & Operations**
- Sales was used to chasing new leads; they weren't sure about selling renewals
- Operations was used to discrete projects; they worried about managing ongoing commitments
- Solution: Involved them early in design. Let them raise concerns. Showed them how this actually makes THEIR jobs easier (Operations gets predictable work, Sales gets easier sales because these are existing relationships). Incentivized the new model in compensation.

## OUTCOMES

### QUANTIFIED RESULTS

**Adoption & Revenue:**
- **Pilot (5 clients):** 5 of 5 converted to subscription (100% adoption)
- **Full rollout (25 clients):** 18 of 25 converted (72% adoption)
- **First-year subscription revenue:** $420,000 annualized (blended average of $2,330/client/month across tiers)
- **Projected revenue at full scale:** $900K+ if remaining 7 clients converted

**Churn & Retention:**
- **Churn before subscription model:** 70% (clients disappeared after first project)
- **Churn during subscription contracts:** ~5% (only 1 of 20 clients churned in year 1, and that was due to budget constraints, not dissatisfaction)
- **Client renewal rates during contract period:** 90%+ (16 of 18 renewed when contracts came up for renewal)

**Revenue Predictability:**
- **Before:** Revenue forecast accuracy was ~40-50% (we were always guessing)
- **After:** Revenue forecast accuracy reached 95% (because 80% of revenue was now contracted, not deal-by-deal)
- **This mattered operationally:** Finance could finally build realistic budgets. The founder could plan hiring and investment with confidence.

**Other Metrics:**
- **Sales cycle:** 2-4 weeks (unchanged, but easier because these were existing relationships)
- **Customer satisfaction:** Average 8.5/10 on post-project surveys (up from ~7/10 before structured feedback)
- **Time spent on sales/acquisition:** Dropped from ~40% of team effort to ~25%
- **Team morale:** Noticeably improved (people weren't constantly stressed about "what's next?")

### Business Impact Beyond the Metrics

**Revenue predictability changed everything:**
- The founder could finally plan hiring without worrying month-to-month. He hired 2 additional delivery people in Q2 2024 because he knew the revenue was there to support them
- Marketing could invest in content and thought leadership because we weren't constantly in crisis-acquisition mode
- Delivery quality improved because teams weren't burning out jumping from new project to new project

**Competitive positioning:**
- We were suddenly different from every other consulting firm in the region
- Prospects would ask, "Do you do ongoing support?" And we could say yes
- This became a key differentiator in RFP responses

**Client lifetime value:**
- Pre-subscription, average client lifetime value was ~$15-20K (one project)
- Post-subscription, clients who adopted subscriptions had LTV trajectories of $40-80K+ (ongoing revenue + upsells to higher tiers)

### Client Feedback

From interviews and NPS feedback:

> *"You've basically become our de facto internal marketing team. It's like having someone on staff without the overhead."* — Manufacturing company (Scale tier client)

> *"This is exactly what we needed. We can now budget for ongoing support and actually execute our strategies instead of planning and then abandoning them."* — Services firm (Growth tier client)

> *"I was skeptical about the monthly fee at first, but when I calculated what we would have paid for ad-hoc projects over a year, the subscription is saving us money AND giving us better results."* — Tech startup (Starter tier, later upgraded to Growth)

### What the Founder Said

> *"I was worried this would complicate things. It's actually the opposite. Now I know what next month's revenue is going to be. I can invest in the team. This is the direction we should have gone 2 years ago."*

## LEARNINGS & REFLECTION

### What I Learned

**1. Data beats assumptions**
- I assumed clients wanted one-off projects. Data proved otherwise.
- The founder assumed clients weren't interested in ongoing relationships. Client research proved otherwise.
- **Lesson:** Before proposing a solution, validate your hypothesis with real data and real conversations.

**2. Positioning can be more powerful than product**
- We didn't invent new services. We repositioned existing capabilities.
- The unlock was helping the market understand what we could do, not building something new.
- **Lesson:** Sometimes the problem isn't your offering; it's how you're describing it to the market.

**3. Pilot programs reduce risk AND buy-in**
- When I proposed "test with 5 clients first," the founder immediately felt comfortable
- By the time we rolled out to all 25, we had proof it worked
- **Lesson:** When proposing big changes, start small, prove it, then scale

**4. Operational clarity matters as much as strategy**
- Having clear definitions (what counts as hours?), clear processes (onboarding checklist), and clear communication (daily dashboards) is what made the model actually work
- Strategy is 20% of the equation; execution is 80%
- **Lesson:** Don't just design the model; design the operations that make it work

**5. Involving your team early prevents resistance**
- When I involved Sales and Operations in designing the model instead of just announcing it, they became advocates instead of skeptics
- **Lesson:** Buy-in is easier when people help build it

### What I'd Do Differently

**1. Involve the delivery team EARLIER**
- I brought Operations in after the founder approved the model
- Next time, I'd have Operations in the room from "Option B vs. Option C" stage so they can raise constraints and shape the design

**2. Build the feedback system BEFORE launch**
- I launched the subscription model and then built the feedback system
- This meant we didn't have data to show ROI of the model in the first month
- Next time, I'd have the feedback system live from day 1 so we can immediately track satisfaction and churn indicators

**3. Create a formal playbook earlier**
- The sales pitch emerged organically through experience
- Next time, I'd document "Here's how you position Starter vs. Growth vs. Scale" and "Here's the Right-Sizing Guide" before the pilot, not after

**4. Segment the pilot more strategically**
- I tested with 5 clients, but didn't segment which 5
- Next time, I'd choose 5 clients deliberately: 1 early-stage, 1 mid-market, 1 enterprise, 1 high-risk, 1 loyal (to test different scenarios and objections)

## CONCLUSION

This project proved that **the best business problems aren't always about building something new—they're about solving a positioning problem, designing an operating model that works, and then executing relentlessly.**

We took a business that was acquiring clients well but losing them immediately after projects, and transformed it into one with recurring revenue, predictable growth, and team stability. The model didn't require new services, new team members, or new technology. It required:
- Clear data showing the problem
- Client research validating the solution
- Operational discipline to make it work
- Communication that helped the market understand what we offered

The result: 100% pilot adoption, 72% full adoption, $420K+ in first-year subscription revenue, 95% forecast accuracy, and a foundation for scaling the business sustainably.
