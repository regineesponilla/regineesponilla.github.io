Portfolio / Case Study #1

Subscription Model Redesign

Transforming a declining business model through strategic subscription architecture.

Top Synergy Management Phils. Inc. (Green Crew House Cleaning Services)
Sales & Marketing Officer
May 2023 – Feb 2024

Executive Summary

This case study demonstrates how systematic problem analysis and solution design can transform a struggling B2B service business. By identifying the root cause of 70% client churn and designing a tiered subscription model, I increased client retention by 90%+, established predictable revenue streams, and created the foundation for scalable growth.

Key Results

  • 70% churn → near 0% during contract periods
  • 100% pilot adoption (5/5 early clients)
  • 72% full rollout success (18/25 target accounts)
  • 50% revenue increase from subscription model
  • 90%+ renewal rates on recurring contracts

Context

Green Crew House Cleaning Services is a recurring deep-cleaning company operating in the Philippines, serving residential and commercial clients. The company generates revenue through one-off cleaning jobs ("project-based delivery"), but faced a critical problem: once a client completed their initial cleaning, 70% never returned for follow-up services.

The Business Challenge

Without recurring revenue, the company was stuck in a "feast or famine" cycle. Each month required aggressive customer acquisition to replace clients who churned. This was expensive, time-consuming, and prevented the business from scaling.

The root cause: clients viewed Green Crew's service as transactional ("I need my house cleaned once"), not recurring ("I want regular deep cleans on a schedule"). There was no mechanism to encourage repeat bookings, no commitment structure, and no incentive for clients to return.

The Problem

70% Annual Client Churn

Green Crew's client base consisted of one-time buyers. After the initial cleaning, clients either:

  • Didn't think they needed another deep clean (underestimated frequency of need)
  • Forgot about the service (no reminder system in place)
  • Shopped around for cheaper competitors (no lock-in, no loyalty incentive)
  • Managed cleaning in-house (perceived it as non-essential ongoing expense)

Revenue Unpredictability

With 70% churn, the company couldn't forecast revenue. Management couldn't confidently plan inventory, staffing, or growth investments. Every month was uncertain.

Unsustainable Growth Model

The company relied entirely on customer acquisition. High-touch sales efforts were required for every booking. Marketing spend was high relative to customer lifetime value. Scaling required proportionally more sales resources.

Solution Design

Tiered Subscription Model

I designed a subscription model that addressed the root causes of churn by making recurring service the default, rather than the exception:

Subscription Tiers

  • Starter: Deep clean every 2 months (6 cleans/year) - for light-use residential
  • Growth: Deep clean monthly (12 cleans/year) - for regular household/small office use
  • Scale: Deep clean every 2 weeks (26 cleans/year) - for high-traffic commercial/large homes

Contract Terms

  • 3-month, 6-month, or 12-month commitment periods
  • Discounted per-clean pricing for longer commitments (10-15% discount on 12-month)
  • Automatic renewal unless client opts out 14 days before expiration

Why This Works

  • Predictability for clients: They know when their home will be cleaned. No need to book each time.
  • Commitment benefit: Clients get better pricing for locking in longer terms. Psychology: "I'm getting a deal."
  • Reduced decision fatigue: Recurring contracts mean fewer booking decisions. Clients simply get their service.
  • Loyalty incentive: Discounts + consistency = reduced churn temptation to switch providers.
  • Predictable revenue: Green Crew can now forecast and plan with confidence.

Implementation

Phase 1: Pilot (Month 1-2)

  • Approached 5 existing high-value clients with the subscription offer
  • Positioned as exclusive beta program with special founding-member pricing
  • Results: 100% pilot adoption (5/5 clients signed subscription contracts)

Phase 2: Rollout (Month 2-6)

  • Expanded to 25 target accounts (mix of existing and new clients)
  • Used pilot success stories in sales conversations
  • Trained sales team on subscription pitch (value-first, not transactional)
  • Results: 72% conversion rate (18/25 accounts signed subscriptions)

Phase 3: Stabilization (Month 6-12)

  • Monitored contract renewals and churn rates
  • Adjusted pricing/tiers based on client feedback
  • Developed referral incentives for existing subscription clients

Results & Business Impact

Quantified Outcomes

  • Churn Rate: 70% → ~5% (near-zero during contract periods)
  • Revenue Stability: 50% increase in recurring revenue month-over-month
  • Client Lifetime Value: Increased 3x through extended contract terms and reduced acquisition costs
  • Client Renewal Rates: 90%+ of subscription contracts renewed on expiry
  • Pilot-to-Rollout Success: 100% adoption in pilot; 72% in full rollout

Strategic Impact

Predictable Revenue: The company shifted from transactional to subscription-based revenue. This allowed management to confidently forecast quarterly revenue and plan growth investments.

Improved Unit Economics: Subscription clients have higher lifetime value. The cost of acquiring a subscription client is amortized over 12+ months rather than recovered in a single transaction.

Scalable Growth Model: The company can now grow by improving retention and expanding contract value, rather than purely increasing customer acquisition.

Reduced Dependency on Sales: Once a client is on a subscription, they continue automatically (low friction). Sales effort shifts from "book each job" to "acquire new subscription clients."

Key Learnings

1. Root Cause Analysis is Essential

I didn't stop at "clients churn." I dug deeper to understand WHY—whether it was price sensitivity, forgotten service, or misaligned expectations. The solution only works because it addresses the actual root cause.

2. Pilot-Test Before Full Rollout

The 5-client pilot was essential. It proved the concept worked, provided success stories, and helped refine the pitch before broader rollout. A 100% pilot adoption rate gave confidence to move forward.

3. Align Incentives

The subscription model works because it aligns incentives: clients want predictable cleaning schedules and discounted pricing; the company wants predictable revenue and reduced churn. Win-win design matters.

4. Communication Matters

Selling "subscription model" feels abstract. But selling "regular deep cleans on a schedule at a discounted rate" feels tangible. Framing the solution in client-benefit language, not company-benefit language, drives adoption.

Conclusion

This case study demonstrates the power of systematic business analysis: identify the real problem, design a solution that addresses root causes, pilot-test to build confidence, and scale with supporting change management.

The subscription model was not a silver bullet, but it was a strategic shift that transformed a transactional business into a recurring-revenue business. That shift unlocked predictable growth, improved unit economics, and created a foundation for scale.

Business Analyst Takeaway: Great solutions come from understanding not just what clients need, but why they need it. Once you know the real problem, the solution often follows logically.
