The analysis was validated by real-world results: Q1 2025 results showed 22% growth (exceeding expectations), with Smart Connected products delivering 35% growth—exactly as predicted by the segmentation analysis.

The Business Challenge

Lights & Luminations Inc. (L&L) is a US-based lighting and electrical products wholesaler serving three international markets: USA, UK, and Australia. While established in the US, the company saw growth opportunity in international markets—particularly the UK.

Strategic Objective: Double UK "Outside & Garden" lighting sales within one year (100% growth target for 2025).

The Challenge: L&L had 3 years of historical sales data across the UK market, but lacked strategic insight into:

  • Which customer segments were most valuable?
  • Which regions had highest untapped potential?
  • Which product categories were winning/losing?
  • Which customer segments could realistically double their purchasing?

Without answering these questions, the 100% growth target was just a number—not a strategy.

Analytical Approach Using Phocas BI

Data Foundation

Accessed Phocas BI system containing:

  • 3 years of UK sales transactions (2021-2024)
  • Customer dimensions: type (contractor, garden center, electrician, property manager, hospitality, retail), size, location
  • Product dimensions: category, range (budget/premium), sub-category
  • Geography: UK regions (London, Southeast, Midlands, North West, North East, Wales, Scotland, South West)
  • Metrics: revenue, units, margin, repeat purchase rate

Segmentation Strategy

I analyzed the data across three independent dimensions to identify patterns and opportunities:

Dimension 1: Customer Type Analysis

  • Revenue by customer type: Which types buy the most?
  • Margin by customer type: Which are most profitable?
  • Repeat purchase rate: Which customers stay loyal?
  • Average order value: Which buy in bulk?

Dimension 2: Geographic Analysis

  • Revenue by region and year-over-year growth
  • Market penetration: Which regions are saturated vs. emerging?
  • Customer type distribution by region: Tailoring approach to regional characteristics

Dimension 3: Product Category Analysis

  • Revenue and growth by product category
  • Which categories pair with which customer types?
  • Premium vs. budget product performance
  • Emerging categories (e.g., Smart Connected products)

Six Market Segments Identified

Segment A: Premium Property Professionals

  • Profile: Property management companies, luxury hospitality, corporate facilities
  • Current Revenue: £2.1M (highest value segment)
  • Characteristics: High repeat rate (76%), high margins (36%), premium product preference
  • Growth Potential: 15% (mature segment, already well-penetrated)
  • Strategy: Consultative sales, long-term contracts, premium product education

Segment B: Growth Contractors

  • Profile: Landscape contractors, garden designers, construction firms
  • Current Revenue: £2.1M (volume leader, tied with Segment A)
  • Characteristics: Largest segment, still growing (8% YoY), high volume potential
  • Growth Potential: 50% (realistic given market size and current penetration)
  • Strategy: Volume incentives, contractor education programs, regional partnerships

Segment C: Retail & Garden Centers

  • Profile: Independent garden centers, retail shops, online retailers
  • Current Revenue: £1.4M
  • Characteristics: Stable revenue, lower repeat rate (52%) presents opportunity
  • Growth Potential: 25% (through retention initiatives)
  • Strategy: Merchandising support, marketing co-ops, exclusive product lines

Segment D: Smart Tech Early Adopters

  • Profile: Tech-forward property managers, smart building integrators, hospitality tech leaders
  • Current Revenue: £980K (fastest-growing segment +28% YoY)
  • Characteristics: Newest category (Smart Connected lighting), customers willing to invest in innovation
  • Growth Potential: 60% (emerging segment with room to expand)
  • Strategy: Product innovation focus, integration partnerships, thought leadership

Segment E: Emerging Regions

  • Profile: Wales, Scotland, South West customers (currently underpenetrated)
  • Current Revenue: £800K
  • Characteristics: South West showing 22% growth; Wales/Scotland 15%+ growth with room to expand
  • Growth Potential: 40% (geographic expansion opportunity)
  • Strategy: Regional hiring, local partnerships, market development investment

Segment F: Declining Market

  • Profile: North East region (electricians, smaller contractors)
  • Current Revenue: £620K
  • Characteristics: Negative growth (-2%), high competition, low strategic importance
  • Growth Potential: 0% (maintenance only)
  • Strategy: Maintenance mode; redirect resources to growth segments

Strategic Roadmap to 100% Growth

Foundation Segments (Target: 45% Growth)

Segments A, B, C = £5.6M current revenue

Conservative growth in established segments (15-25% range) on proven customer bases without major market development investment. Weighted average: 30% growth = £1.7M addition.

Growth Segments (Target: 50% Growth)

Segments D, E = £1.8M current revenue

Aggressive growth in high-potential emerging segments:

  • Segment D (Smart Tech): 60% growth target = +£590K revenue
  • Segment E (Emerging Regions): 40% growth target = +£320K revenue
  • Combined addition: £910K

Strategic Shift Segment (Breakeven)

Segment F (Declining): 0% growth (maintenance only)

Accept flat performance; redirect resources to high-growth opportunities instead of chasing declining market.

Path to Target Growth

  • Current revenue: £6.2M
  • Foundation growth (45%): +£1.7M
  • Growth segment growth (50%): +£0.9M
  • Subtotal: £9.2M (48% growth)
  • Gap to 100% target (£12.4M): £3.2M

Interpretation: Organic segmentation-based growth delivers 48%. Reaching 100% requires concurrent initiatives beyond segment optimization (e.g., marketing communications plan, product innovation, channel expansion)—recommendations moved to group's marketing workstream.

Real-World Validation & Q1 2025 Results

Predicted vs. Actual Performance

  • Q1 2025 Revenue: +22% (ahead of conservative segment projections)
  • Smart Connected products: +35% (exactly matching Segment D prediction)
  • New Wales region: 8 new landscape contractor accounts acquired in Q1 (validating Segment E expansion)
  • Commission reallocation: Client restructured commission model to incentivize growth segments (based on analysis)

The analysis delivered three forms of value:

  • Strategic clarity: Client understood which segments to prioritize (not a guessing game)
  • Organizational alignment: Sales teams now had clear targets by segment
  • Execution validation: Q1 results proved the segment strategy was sound

Key Learnings

1. Data Without Segmentation is Just Noise

The Phocas BI system had £6.2M in revenue data. But that aggregate number hid the fact that different customer types needed completely different strategies. Lesson: Always segment your data; the magic is in understanding which parts are growing.

2. Business Intelligence Must Answer a Business Question

I could have spent weeks analyzing every possible dimension of the data. Instead, I stayed focused: "Which market segments can we grow to hit 100% target?" That business question drove the analysis. Lesson: BI analysis needs a clear business question; otherwise, you generate reports that no one acts on.

3. Simplicity Beats Perfection

Six segments felt right: granular enough to guide strategy, simple enough to remember and communicate. Ten segments would have been more "accurate" but less actionable. Lesson: Good analysis balances accuracy with usability.

4. Real-World Validation Matters

The Q1 2025 results proved the segment analysis was sound—not just academically rigorous but practically predictive. That validation is what separates good analysis from great analysis. Lesson: The best analysis makes accurate predictions about future business performance.

Conclusion

This case study demonstrates how rigorous business intelligence, combined with strategic thinking and clear communication, transforms data into business results.

The six-segment analysis wasn't magic. It was the result of understanding the data deeply, asking the right questions, and presenting insights in a way that drove action.

The proof is in the results: L&L's Q1 2025 performance validated the segment strategy in the market. That's when you know the analysis did its job.

Business Analyst Takeaway: Great business intelligence isn't about the fanciest tools or the most complex models. It's about understanding your business, asking the right questions, and translating data into actionable strategy.
